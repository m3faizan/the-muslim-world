About Bani Unaif Mosque

On the northern outskirts of Madinah, within the historic dwellings of the Bani Unaif tribe, stands a mosque that preserves the trace of a noble visit by the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him). Its story dates back to the second year after the Hijrah, when the Prophet (peace be upon him) visited his companion Talhah ibn Al-Bara’ ibn Ubayr ibn Wabra (may Allah be pleased with him), a young man from the Bani Unaif tribe who had fallen ill.

During this visit, the Prophet (peace be upon him) prayed near one of their forts, and the tribe carefully preserved the place where he prayed. They sprinkled the ground with water to protect its mark until they built a mosque over it, a lasting symbol of love, reverence, and devotion.

Early Architectural Description
The original structure of Masjid Bani Unaif was modest in scale and design, covering around 35 square meters. It consisted of four basalt stone walls about two meters high and sixty centimeters thick, with no roof.

At its center was a simple mihrab framed by neatly hewn basalt stones, and its entrance faced north. This straightforward form reflected the humility of early Islamic architecture in Madinah, purposeful, enduring, and built purely for worship.

Restoration and Preservation
In recognition of its deep spiritual and historical value, the saudi government undertook a careful restoration of Bani Unaif mosque in 1442 AH (2020 CE), preserving its original stonework while enhancing its structural integrity.

 

The restoration works included:

Reinforcing the original basalt walls with supporting arches inspired by traditional Madinah architecture.
Installing a wooden-covered iron roof structure for protection and stability.
Creating a landscaped courtyard surrounded by a stone wall adorned with heritage-style arches.
 

Further site improvements were completed in 1445 AH (2024 CE), providing visitor parking and upgraded access routes around the mosque.

Religious and Historical Significance
Among the earliest mosques where the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) prayed.
A symbol of the Prophet’s kindness, connection, and compassion toward his companions and the people of Madinah.
An authentic representation of the city’s early mosque architecture, simple yet deeply spiritual.
A reflection of saudi’s ongoing commitment to preserving and documenting the Prophet’s heritage for future generations.
