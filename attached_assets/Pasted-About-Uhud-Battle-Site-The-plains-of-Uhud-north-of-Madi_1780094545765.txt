About Uhud Battle Site

The plains of Uhud, north of Madinah, are etched with the memory of heroism and sacrifice. In the third year of Hijrah (625 CE), the Muslims, led by the Prophet (peace be upon him), faced the Quraysh army in a battle that tested patience, faith, and resilience. This encounter left an enduring mark on Islamic history, symbolizing steadfastness even in the face of adversity.

Surrounding Terrain and Landmarks

Wadi Qana
Flowing from the northeast past Mount Uhud toward the northwest, Wadi Qana joins Wadi Bathan and later Wadi Al-‘Aqiq. During the battle, floodwaters in 49 AH (669 CE) uncovered some of the martyrs’ graves, which were moved to higher ground, now known as the Martyrs’ Cemetery of Uhud.


 Jabal Al-Rumah (The Archers’ Hill)
Located approximately 3.5 km north of the Prophet’s Mosque, this hill measures 180 m in length and 40 m in width. The Prophet (peace be upon him) positioned fifty archers here to safeguard the Muslim army’s rear. When some left their posts prematurely, the battle’s outcome shifted, turning the hill into a timeless symbol of discipline, obedience, and steadfastness.


Martyrs’ Cemetery of Uhud
The cemetery contains the remains of over seventy companions, including Hamza ibn Abdul-Muttalib (may Allah be pleased with him), the Prophet’s  (peace be upon him) uncle and “Chief of Martyrs.” The Prophet (peace be upon him) visited and prayed for them. The Saudi government has preserved this sacred site with protective boundaries.


Mount Uhud
Famous for its reddish rocks of rhyolite, dacite, and andesite, Mount Uhud rises 5 km north of the Prophet’s Mosque, stretching 7 km long and 2–3 km wide. Its hollows, known as Al-Maharīs, collect rainwater, and its slopes are rich with over 80 plant species, including Al-‘Awsaj, Sumar, Hamham, Naqi’, and Harmal, creating a landscape both beautiful and historically significant.


Shiʿb Al-Jirār
A narrow mountain pass through Mount Uhud that channels Wadi Al-Jirār into the battlefield. Tradition holds that the Prophet (peace be upon him) used this pass during the battle, witnessing the unwavering courage of his companions. Today, it stands as a lasting symbol of faith and resilience.
