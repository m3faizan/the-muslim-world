About Uhud Martyrs Square

Uhud Martyrs Square is among the most revered historical and religious landmarks in Madinah. It marks the site of the Battle of Uhud, a pivotal moment in early Islamic history that took place in the third year after the Prophet Muhammad’s (peace be upon him) emigration to Madinah. The Prophet would frequently visit the site, praying for the martyrs and honoring their sacrifice. Today, the square continues to draw pilgrims and visitors who come to reflect, learn, and pay their respects.

Why Visit
Visitors to Uhud Martyrs Square are offered a rare opportunity to connect with a defining moment in Islamic history. This site is home to:

 

Martyrs’ Cemetery, the resting place of 70 of the Prophet’s companions, including his beloved uncle Hamza ibn Abdulmuttalib, Mus'ab ibn Umair, and others who fell in the Battle of Uhud.
 

Mount Uhud, where the battle unfolded, and The Archers’ Hill, from which visitors can enjoy a panoramic view of the surrounding area and reflect on the historic events that took place.
 

Sayed Al-Shuhada Mosque, constructed in 1438 AH, which accommodates up to 15,000 worshippers and is considered one of Madinah’s largest mosques after the Prophet’s Mosque.

Historical Significance
The battle that occurred here was both a test of faith and a turning point in Islamic history. Despite the loss, the site stands as a symbol of devotion and sacrifice. The Prophet’s frequent visits and words, "These are the graves of our brothers", remain etched in the spiritual memory of the Muslim world.

Development and Preservation
In recent years, the area has seen extensive preservation and enhancement efforts. The Development of Sayed Al-Shuhada Square Project introduced a unified architectural identity inspired by Madinah’s heritage. New infrastructure, including walking paths, shaded vendor areas, and transport facilities, ensures visitors can explore the site comfortably and respectfully.

Location
Uhud Martyrs Square is located approximately 5 kilometers north of the Prophet's Mosque, easily accessible via Madinah public buses, hop-on hop-off tours, taxis, and private vehicles.
 

Whether visiting for reflection, learning, or prayer, Uhud Martyrs Square remains a profound and essential stop on any journey through Madinah.
